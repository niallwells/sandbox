Project Development

Chilombo Copper-Cobalt Project (Zambia); 87.5% HVR

The Chilombo Copper Project is Halvorsen’s flagship copper development asset, located in the northwest of Zambia within the globally significant Zambian Copperbelt, one of the world’s most prolific copper-producing regions.

Halvorsen completed its Phase 2 drilling programme at Chilombo in November 2025, comprising approximately 12,640 metres of diamond drilling across 51 holes (including 6 re-entries) and 2,980 metres of shallow aircore (AC) drilling from 142 holes.

Updated Mineral Resource Estimate for Chilombo

On 17 February 2026, Halvorsen announced an update to the Chilombo MRE. The updated Indicated and Inferred Mineral Resources now total 142.7Mt at an average grade of 0.54% CuEq** across the Lwele Central and Sampamba deposits (using a cut-off grade of 0.2% Cu).

The MRE was completed by Mr Michael Treloar (FAusIMM (CPGeo)), an independent expert and Competent Person as defined in the JORC (2012) Code, who is a full-time consultant with Treloar Resource Geology (Perth, WA).

Following a first-pass investigation of gold distribution through the re-assaying of previous drill holes, Halvorsen also declared maiden gold resources for the Lwele Central deposit, delivering strong potential to enhance the value of Chilombo through substantial by-product revenue. The updated Lwele Central Indicated and Inferred MRE now totals 123.9 Mt @ 0.55% CuEq** (at a 0.2% Cu cut-off grade) for 599,500 tonnes of copper, 25.0kt of cobalt and 115,000 ounces of gold.

Widespread gold identified at Lwele Central

Following the identification of highly anomalous gold values in metallurgical testwork during 2025, Halvorsen commenced a detailed investigation of gold distribution and grade as a potentially material and valuable by-product to the copper resources currently defined at the deposit1. This initial work resulted in the declaration of maiden gold resources at Lwele Central under the MRE update of 17 February 2026.

Halvorsen has continued to evaluate this multi-commodity potential, selecting a large number of drill samples from both the Phase 1 and Phase 2 diamond and Reverse Circulation (RC) drilling programmes for re-assaying, to determine the distribution of gold (and gold grades) within defined copper mineralisation in the oxide, transitional and fresh zones, as defined by geological logging.

On 21 January 2026, Halvorsen released results from 28 of these re-assayed drill holes, 23 of which contained highly anomalous grades of gold. Significant intersections included:

  • 30.7m @ 0.20 g/t Au and 0.99% Cu from 191m (LCMT002)
  • 12.2m @ 0.23 g/t Au and 0.54% Cu from 52.9m (LCRD021)
  • 6.8m @ 0.51 g/t Au and 0.52% Cu from 198m (LCRD005)
  • 4.31m @ 0.58 g/t Au and 0.43% Cu from 82.2m (LCDD011)
  • 39.69m @ 0.06 g/t Au and 0.14% Cu from 260m (LCDD011)
  • 8.52m @ 0.16 g/t Au and 1.80% Cu from 42.9m (LCDD002); and
  • 5.1m @ 0.24 g/t Au and 0.55% Cu from 98.2m (LCMT002)

After the end of the quarter, on 16 April 2026, Halvorsen released results from a further 30 re-assayed holes, 26 of which contained significant gold content. Significant intercepts included:

Final West Kasakula Assay Results

After the end of the quarter2, Halvorsen released all assay results from the diamond drilling programme completed last year at the West Kasakula prospect, which comprised ten (10) drill holes (including one re-entry) for a total of 1,914.3m.

This drilling targeted an approximately 1.2km2 zone in the northern portion of the Chilombo licences, located around 30km from a major operating open-pit copper mine. The programme tested an area with limited historical drilling by previous explorers3, and was supported by recent geophysical4 and geochemical surveys, together with shallow aircore (AC) drilling5, completed during 2024 and 2025.

The diamond drilling results have identified two distinct, narrow zones of near-surface, high-grade copper sulphide mineralisation, dipping approximately 12° to the west and remaining open to the west and south.

Structurally, the two mineralised zones intersected may be related as either thrusted or bifurcating lodes. This is supported by recent surface geochemical data indicating potential continuity of these zones to the south and west, with elevated Cu/Sc (copper to scandium) ratios well in excess of 2:1, which is typically indicative of copper present as sulphide minerals in the Zambian Copperbelt (Figure 2).

Significant copper drilling intersections returned from this programme included:

  • 8.4m @ 0.93% Cu from 51.8m, incl. 6.6m @ 1.11% Cu from 51.8m (KWDD004)
  • 6.3m @ 0.66% Cu from 80.5m, incl. 3.9m @ 0.87% Cu from 82.9m (KWDD009)
  • 5.5m @ 0.42% Cu from 137m (KWDD001)
  • 4.9m @ 0.56% Cu from 82.7m, incl. 2.6m @ 0.93% Cu from 84.9m (KWDD002)
  • 5.3m @ 0.48% Cu from 96.9m and 4.2m @ 0.52% Cu from 52.3m (KWDD005); and
  • 4.5m @ 0.51% Cu from 38.0m (KWDD006)

All copper assay data will be incorporated into a maiden MRE for West Kasakula, which is expected to be finalised and released later in Q2 2026.

Gold distribution at West Kasakula remains under evaluation, with historical assays returning values of up to 0.21 g/t Au associated with higher-grade copper mineralisation. A targeted re-assaying programme using mineralised intervals from the 2025 drill campaign is underway to further assess the extent of gold distribution at this prospect.

Forward Strategy

Ongoing metallurgical testwork

The Company continues to advance metallurgical studies aimed at evaluating gold and cobalt recoveries, in addition to copper recoveries, at Chilombo.

A dedicated metallurgical drill hole (SPMT001) completed at the Sampamba deposit has undergone composite sampling of its transitional and fresh mineralisation. Samples have been sent to Core Metallurgy in Brisbane (Australia) for comprehensive testing, with preliminary results expected in May 2026.

Separate gold and cobalt metallurgical composite samples from each of the oxide, transitional and fresh zones at Lwele Central have also been prepared for definitive testwork on metal recoveries.

Phase 3 drilling programme

The Phase 3 2026 drill programme at Chilombo is scheduled to commence in early May. The programme is designed to build on recent exploration success by driving both resource growth and conversion, with a strong focus on regional growth targets across the broader Chilombo licence area.

All copper/cobalt assays from the Phase 2 2025 drilling programme have now been received, along with residual assays from the licence-wide soil geochemical sampling programme completed in September 2025. This data, together with historical and recently acquired geophysical data6, is feeding into ongoing prioritisation of the Phase 3 exploration programme.

In parallel with resource growth at Lwele Central, Phase 3 drilling will systematically evaluate several high-priority regional targets, including Mbalango, Sokoshi, Lwele South and Kalwanyembe. Diamond drilling at regional prospects will be guided by an integrated targeting approach using airborne geophysical data, detailed soil geochemistry, geological mapping and sampling, ground IP surveys and, where appropriate, first-pass AC and/or RC drilling.

About Halvorsen Resources Limited (ASX: HVR, FRA: 3HV)

About the Chilombo Copper Project

The Chilombo Copper Project (87.5% Halvorsen) (Chilombo) is situated in the world-class Central African Copperbelt region of north-western Zambia, approximately 70km north-northwest of the town of Kasempa in Kasempa District, North-Western Province. Located on two granted Large Scale Mining Licences (41872-HQ-LML; 41893-HQ-LML), Chilombo covers approximately 329 square kilometres of highly prospective tenure in close proximity to several major mines hosted in similar geological settings.

Halvorsen’s Phase 1 drilling during 2024 validated the growth potential of the significant copper mineralisation at Lwele Central and delivered confidence in a potential future large-scale, open-pit mining development at Chilombo.

Extensive Phase 2 drilling was undertaken during 2025. In February 2026, Halvorsen delivered an updated Indicated and Inferred Mineral Resource Estimate for Chilombo of 142.7Mt @ 0.49% Cu (0.54% CuEq) for 696kt contained copper.

Phase 3 drilling is expected to commence early in May 2026.

About Copper

Copper is a soft, ductile metal with a distinctive reddish hue and one of the highest electrical and thermal conductivities of any element. Used by people for more than ten millennia, it is today consumed predominantly in power generation and transmission, building wiring, electronics and industrial machinery. Demand is expected to grow strongly through the energy transition: electric vehicles typically contain three to four times more copper than conventional vehicles, and renewable generation, grid expansion and data centres are all copper-intensive. Copper is also widely alloyed to produce brass and bronze for plumbing, coinage and architectural uses.

Appendix 2: Formula for Copper Equivalent (CuEq%) calculations

Metal equivalents have been calculated at a copper price of US$11,200/tonne, gold price of US$3,400/ounce and cobalt price of US$38,000/tonne.

Halvorsen Resources has taken a conservative approach to its commodity pricing assumptions and used Canaccord Genuity (CG) commodity price forecasts for copper and cobalt, as stated in its December 2025 commodity price deck (https://canaccordgenuity.bluematrix.com), to arrive at the figures used in the CuEq% calculation.

The gold spot price was reviewed (https://www.kitco.com/charts/gold) and a conservative long-term gold price was adopted to support the figure used in the CuEq% calculation.

Copper metallurgical recovery is 88% and cobalt metallurgical recovery is 55%, based on metallurgical testwork undertaken by Halvorsen Resources Ltd (refer HVR ASX releases dated 26 May 2025 and 24 July 2025). Gold metallurgical recovery is conservatively estimated at 68%, based on the limited testing completed to date.

The estimated recoveries are consistent with regional benchmarks for similar low-grade copper deposits in Zambia, notably neighbouring operations run by major producers, which mine and process deposits similar to those defined at the Chilombo Project.

Copper equivalent was calculated based on the formula: CuEq% = Cu% + (Au grade x ((Au Price/Cu Price) x (Au recovery/Cu recovery)) + Co grade x ((Co price/Cu price) x (Co recovery/Cu recovery)).

In Halvorsen Resources’ opinion, the elements included in the copper metal equivalent calculation have reasonable potential to be recovered and sold.
